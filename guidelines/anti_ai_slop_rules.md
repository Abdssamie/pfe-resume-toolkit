# Anti-AI-Slop & Modern ATS Resume Guidelines 🛡️

This document establishes non-negotiable standards for creating resumes that sound **human, credible, and grounded**, while scoring high on **Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)**.

---

## 1. The Core Philosophy

Recruiters and hiring managers read dozens of resumes daily. They immediately recognize AI-generated text by its generic tone, inflated corporate buzzwords, and vague accomplishments that sound impressive but say nothing.

> **Golden Rule**: Every bullet point must describe **what you actually built or solved**, **the exact tools/technologies used**, and **the concrete outcome or metric**. If a bullet point could apply to 10,000 other candidates without changing a word, it is AI slop and must be rewritten.

---

## 2. Forbidden AI Buzzwords vs. Grounded Engineering Language

| ❌ Forbidden AI Slop | Why It Fails | ✅ Grounded Engineering Replacement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *"Spearheaded cutting-edge cross-functional initiatives"* | Empty corporate jargon; describes no actual work. | *"Architected and deployed an automated data pipeline using Python and PostgreSQL."* |
| *"Leveraged state-of-the-art AI to synergize workflows"* | Buzzword soup; hides what the model or script actually did. | *"Built a Scikit-Learn classification model achieving 98% accuracy to predict defect rates."* |
| *"Demonstrated strong leadership and dynamic problem-solving"* | Self-praise without evidence. | Let the accomplishments prove it: *"Coordinated 4 peer engineers across a 6-week sprint to deliver..."* |
| *"Passionate, results-driven, detail-oriented professional"* | Cliche filler that takes up prime resume real estate. | State facts: *"Final-year engineering student with 2 production deployments and 3 published open-source tools."* |
| *"Assisted in various tasks and supported the team"* | Sounds passive and timid. | Focus on your ownership: *"Engineered automated validation scripts reducing manual data entry errors."* |
| *"Pioneered the transformative evolution of..."* | Melodramatic inflation. | *"Designed and implemented RESTful endpoints in ASP.NET Core consumed by 200+ daily users."* |

## 3. The Formula for High-Impact Bullet Points

Every bullet point should follow Google's **XYZ Formula** or the **Action + Tool + Outcome** framework:

$$\text{Accomplished } [X] \text{ as measured by } [Y] \text{ by doing } [Z]$$

### Examples:
- **Weak / AI Slop**: *"Responsible for testing water samples and ensuring quality standards were met."*
- **Strong & Technical**: *"Conducted physico-chemical compliance analyses (ICP-OES, UV-Vis spectrophotometry) across 150+ regional water batches following Moroccan NM 03.7.001 regulatory standards."*

- **Weak / AI Slop**: *"Developed a modern and intuitive web application for laboratory management."*
- **Strong & Technical**: *"Architected an enterprise laboratory portal in React and ASP.NET Core, automating sample registration, Certificate of Analysis generation, and eliminating error-prone spreadsheets."*

## 4. Academic Honesty & Coursework vs. Self-Taught Distinction

One of the biggest mistakes AI agents make is fabricating that a university taught modern technologies (e.g., claiming a chemistry school taught React and Docker).

1. **Academic Coursework**:
   - Only list subjects **actually taught** in the curriculum (e.g., C, Python, MATLAB, Applied Mathematics, Heat Transfer, Organic Synthesis).
   - If applying for tech roles from an engineering background, emphasize common foundations: Algorithms, Differential Equation Solvers, Numerical Analysis, Process Dynamics, and Statistical Modeling.
2. **Self-Taught / Open-Source / Practical Skills**:
   - Explicitly separate modern web frameworks, cloud deployments, and specialized tools into **Projects**, **Open-Source**, or **Technical Skills**.
   - Back self-taught claims with **live links** (GitHub repositories, live demos, packages published on npm/PyPI, or active servers).

## 5. ATS (Applicant Tracking System) Compliance Rules

Modern ATS scanners parse resumes into text before a human reads them. Formatting mistakes can cause parsers to corrupt or discard applications.

1. **Single-Column Flow for Core Content**: Multi-column tables often confuse parsers into reading text out of order. Standard section sequences (`Header` → `Summary` → `Education` → `Experience` → `Projects` → `Skills`) ensure 100% parse accuracy.
2. **Standard Section Headings**: Use predictable titles: `Education`, `Experience`, `Projects`, `Skills`, `Certifications`. Avoid creative titles like *"My Journey"* or *"Where I've Been"*.
3. **Parseable Dates and Locations**: Use standard format: `July 2026 – Aug 2026` and `City, Country`.
4. **Standard Web-Safe Fonts**: Use clean serif or sans-serif fonts (Liberation Serif, Helvetica, Arial, Times New Roman, Roboto).
5. **No Visual Fluff**: Avoid embedding text inside images, graphic skill bars (e.g., "Python 85%"), or complex nested tables.

---

## 6. Page Length and Formatting Strictness

- **Students / PFE Candidates / New Graduates**:
  - **1 Page** is ideal for general software, business, and junior engineering applications.
  - **Strictly 2 Pages** is permitted ONLY if the candidate has substantial verified internships, multiple production projects, publications, or dual-discipline backgrounds.
- **Forbidden**: Never submit a resume that is 1 page and 3 stray lines spilling onto page 2. It must be **exactly 1 full page** or **exactly 2 full pages**.
